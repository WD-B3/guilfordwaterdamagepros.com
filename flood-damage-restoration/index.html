Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup in Guilford, IN

Rising creek water, groundwater and surface flooding carry silt and bacteria. The cleanup is heavier than a pipe leak, and the first steps are about safety.

Flood damage restoration is the removal, cleaning and drying of a building after rising water from a creek, river, groundwater or surface runoff has entered it. In Guilford the usual source is Tanners Creek, which forks at the center of the community. Floodwater is treated as contaminated, so the work differs from a clean pipe leak. If water is in your home now, call the 24/7 emergency line and stay out of it until the power question is settled.

Leave the building if water is near outlets, the panel or a gas appliance. Do not drive through flooded roads, and do not wade into dark water. If you can reach a dry spot, photograph the waterline before anything is moved.

When floodwater is an emergency, and it almost always is

Moving water brings sediment, bacteria and chemicals that settle into every porous material it touches. Carpet, pad, insulation and the lower part of drywall usually cannot be cleaned and have to be removed. The sooner the structure is opened and dried, the smaller the replacement list.

Calls come in all night during creek events, and we offer same-day response to addresses a free crew can reach. After the water recedes, the clock keeps running, because the wet structure is the part that grows mold if it stays damp.

Guilford, Tanners Creek and the Ohio River

Guilford was platted in 1850 at the forks of Tanners Creek, and the creek still shapes the community. In 2019 heavy overnight rain pushed it into homes, and local reporting described a water rescue at a house close to the creek. A resident who has lived in town for decades said it was the worst flooding since 1997 or 1998. The covered bridge park near State Road 1 has also taken floodwater.

The Ohio River is a different kind of flood. Lawrenceburg is protected by a levee system, and the US 50 floodgate at Tanners Creek is part of that protection. In the flood of 1937 the levee then in place gave way, and water stood in the town for about two weeks. Flood risk is also not limited to mapped areas, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask about a flood policy well before the rain.

The Indiana Department of Insurance notes that homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, and that a new flood policy does not normally take effect until 30 days after purchase. Ask your agent what applies to your policy. For cost, call the line and describe it.

Document before you discard. Photograph the waterline, the damaged contents and the rooms, keep receipts, and ask your insurer what they require.

Common questions

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. A same-day visit is offered when your address fits an open slot, and the dispatcher will tell you what to do about power and safety while you wait.

Why is floodwater treated differently from a pipe leak?

Floodwater picks up bacteria, silt and chemicals from the ground and the creek. That is why porous materials are removed, not dried in place.

Does homeowners insurance cover a creek flood?

Usually not. Homeowners policies generally exclude flood, and a separate flood policy is needed. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and note that new flood policies normally have a 30-day waiting period.

How do I check whether my property is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your address, and ask the Dearborn County Building Department about floodplain rules for your property.
