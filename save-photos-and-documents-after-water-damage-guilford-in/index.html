Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

Wet paper is fragile and gets worse with time. The first move is to stop it from drying stuck together or growing mold.

Photos and documents are the items people most regret losing after a leak. They are also the ones that respond best to quick action. Paper survives a soaking if it is handled gently and dried or frozen before it molds. Here is what to do, and what to avoid.

First steps, in order

  1. Make sure the area is safe and the water source is stopped.
  2. Wear gloves if the water was dirty, and handle items by the edges.
  3. Do not pull stuck photos or pages apart when they are wet.
  4. Move items out of standing water to a dry, cool place with moving air.
  5. If you cannot dry them within a day or two, freeze them to stop damage.

Photos

Prints that are still wet can be rinsed gently in clean water if mud or dirt is on them, then laid face up on a clean towel or paper to dry. Do not stack them. Albums with plastic sleeves are best opened and air dried. If photos have stuck together, freeze them and ask a conservator for help rather than pulling them apart.

Papers and books

Place paper towels or blotter paper between pages and replace them as they soak up water. Stand books on their ends with the pages fanned out, in a room with a dehumidifier. For valuable documents, make copies before you start, because paper can be fragile once it dries.

Prevention for next time

Keep irreplaceable items off the floor, in sealed plastic bins on shelves rather than in cardboard boxes in basements. Scan key documents and photos to a cloud service. A flood or leak happens quickly, and a stored copy is the best protection.

How to freeze wet papers properly

Freezing buys time. It stops mold and ink bleed until you can thaw and dry items one at a time. Put photos and papers in freezer bags or wrap them in wax paper, keep batches small, and label each bag with the contents. A standard freezer works for most household items.

Do not freeze items that have already begun to mold unless you can handle them with gloves and a mask. Do not microwave or oven-dry anything. If the collection is large, a document recovery service can freeze-dry it under controlled conditions.

Digital backups save the day

A cloud copy or a second drive kept away from home is the best protection for irreplaceable files. Phones can scan key papers in a few minutes. The time you spend now is far less than the time a replacement request takes later.

What to do with certificates and legal papers

Passports, deeds, titles, insurance policies and wills should be copied or scanned as soon as they are dry enough to handle. Contact the issuing office for replacements of anything that cannot be saved. Keep a list of what was damaged and when you requested each replacement, since some offices need proof of loss.

Salvaging framed art and keepsakes

Framed pictures should be taken apart carefully. Remove the backing, lift the print and lay it out to dry. Glass that touched the print can stick, so move slowly. For valuable or old items, a conservator can advise before you do anything. Keepsakes made of fabric can be washed gently if the water was clean and dried flat.

Record what you saved and what was lost. A short list with photos supports a claim and helps you decide which items deserve professional attention.

Work in small batches and keep a clean, flat surface ready for drying. Rushing a large stack leads to tears and stuck pages. If the pile grows faster than you can handle, freeze the rest and work through it over several days.

Common questions

Can I freeze wet photos at home in Guilford?

Yes. Put them in a freezer bag, separated with wax paper if possible, and freeze them until you can get help.

Should I use a hair dryer on wet papers?

No. Heat can warp paper and set stains. Use air movement and blotting.

What about wet laptops and phones?

Power them off, remove the battery if you can, and do not turn them on. See a repair professional.

How do I replace lost vital records?

Contact the county or state office that issued them. Photographs of the originals help.
