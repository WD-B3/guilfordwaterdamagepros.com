Guilford and the creek forks

Guilford was platted in 1850 at the forks of Tanners Creek. The Guilford Covered Bridge Park, where the 1879 bridge stands at State Road 1 and York Ridge, is the last covered bridge in Dearborn County and a useful landmark when you describe your location to a dispatcher. Creek water and plumbing leaks are the two main water sources here. See flood damage restoration and burst pipe water damage.

How each town differs

  • Lawrenceburg is the county seat on the Ohio River, with older downtown buildings and large commercial sites.
  • Aurora has a historic downtown and a hillside above the river, so water arrives from the river side and from the roof side.
  • Moores Hill sits on a ridge at a listed 994 feet, so storm water and plumbing matter more than river water.
  • West Harrison is a small town against the Ohio line, with household plumbing failures as the main calls.

Dillsboro, ZIP 47018, is also in Dearborn County, and calls from there reach the same line. If your town is not listed, call and give the address. Coverage is decided by whether a professional is available for your location, not by a printed list.

What to have ready when you call

  1. The full street address, town and ZIP.
  2. Where the water came from, if you know.
  3. Which rooms or floors are wet and how deep the water is.
  4. Whether anyone is in danger or power is involved.

How the line decides who goes where

Every call is answered by the same emergency line. When you call, you are asked for your address, the source of the water and what it has reached. That tells the team how urgent the job is, what equipment to bring and how to plan the route. Distance matters, but severity matters more.

Same-day response is available when the schedule allows for your street. During storms or cold snaps, many people call at once, and jobs with active flooding or safety risks come first. The team tells you honestly what to expect on the call, and what to do in the meantime.

Giving a clear address

Small towns and rural roads can make addresses hard to find. Give the street number, the street, the town and the ZIP. Add the nearest cross street or a landmark, such as the covered bridge park in Guilford, and note any gate, long driveway or hidden entrance. If your mailing town differs from where the house sits, say both.

Homes, rentals and businesses

The same line takes calls from homeowners, landlords, property managers and business owners. Rental properties need a record of who authorized the work, and commercial jobs need a plan that keeps the business open where possible. Tell the team which applies, and see commercial water damage restoration for business losses.

If your town is not listed

The list is a guide to the towns with their own pages, not a fence. Callers from other Dearborn County towns, such as Dillsboro, reach the same line. If you are farther away, call, give the address and ask whether a professional is available for your location. An honest answer on the phone is better than a promise on a web page.

Why we list straight-line distances

The distances in the table are measured in a straight line from ZIP centers, because that is a number anyone can check. Road distances are longer and vary with weather and traffic, so we do not publish drive times as promises. When you call, we tell you honestly how soon a crew can reach your address.

Listed elevations run from 515 feet in Aurora to 994 feet in Moores Hill, so routes in this area climb and drop. Crews plan around that, and your description of the water helps decide what to bring and in what order.
