The first 24 to 72 hours decide the repair bill

Water moves faster than most people expect. Within minutes it wicks up drywall, follows wiring and pipe chases, and drops into the floor cavity below. Indiana's own insurance regulator warns that wet items can start growing mold within 24 to 48 hours, which is why speed matters more than polish in the first day.

Early work is simple and physical. A professional shuts off the source if it is still running, pulls standing water with pumps or a truck-mounted extraction unit, and takes moisture readings in every room. Those readings are the record of what was wet and when.

Drying then runs as a closed system. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces while low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air, and the equipment is checked and adjusted each day. The job ends when the readings say it is dry, not when a calendar says so.

Local conditions

Why Guilford homes get water in more than one way

Guilford is a small unincorporated community in York Township, Dearborn County, platted in 1850 at the forks of Tanners Creek. That location explains most of the water calls here. A creek that rises quickly after heavy rain can reach homes along its banks, while the older and newer houses on higher ground deal with the usual plumbing, roof and appliance failures.

Tanners Creek has come into Guilford homes before. In 2019 heavy overnight rain pushed the creek into houses in the community, and one home close to the water needed a rescue. A longtime resident told local reporters it was the worst flooding she had seen in town since 1997 or 1998. If your lower level has ever taken creek water, read the flood damage page before the next storm season.

The Guilford Covered Bridge Park, where the 1879 bridge now stands at State Road 1 and York Ridge, sits close to the same creek. It is the last covered bridge left in Dearborn County, and it is a handy landmark when you describe where you are to a dispatcher. For a property near the creek, the basement cleanup page covers what happens when a sump pump cannot keep up.

Services by cause

All 19 services, sorted by where the water came from

Pipes and plumbing. When the water comes from inside the walls, the source decides the work. A burst pipe floods fast, a frozen pipe often fails when it thaws, a leaking water heater can empty a whole tank, and a slab leak hides under the floor until the bill or the warm spot gives it away.

Appliances and HVAC. Most appliance floods come from a worn hose or drain line, which is covered under appliance leak water damage. A clogged condensate line causes an AC leak that stains ceilings, and the kitchen has its own cabinet and sink-base problems.

Weather and building envelope. A leaking roof shows up first as a ceiling stain or sag. Wind and rain events fall under storm water damage, rising water under flood damage, wet lower levels under basement water damage and damp under-floor voids under crawl space water damage.

Water removal and drying. Pumping comes first with water extraction, and the careful part is structural drying and dehumidification, which includes moisture mapping. Contaminated loss belongs to sewage backup cleanup.

Floors and walls. The material that got wet sets the plan. See hardwood floors, carpet and pad and walls and drywall. Businesses and multi-unit buildings are covered by commercial water damage restoration.

Emergency water damage

What to do while you wait for help

An emergency does not pause for a callback, so some of the most useful steps are the ones you can take before anyone arrives. None of them requires special equipment, and all of them reduce what has to be removed later.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff at the fixture or the main water valve. If you do not know where it is, finding it now is the best use of the next minute.

Power and gas first

If water touches outlets, appliances or the panel, do not wade in. Leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

Lift what you can

Move electronics, papers, shoes and small furniture to dry surfaces. Do not carry heavy items through standing water.

Take photos

Photograph the water, the source and the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

Keep people and pets out

Water that came from a drain, toilet or creek can carry bacteria. Treat it as contaminated until a professional says otherwise.

Call the 24/7 line

The dispatcher will ask about the source, the rooms and how long the water has been there, then explain what happens next.

Repair after drying

Water damage repair in Guilford after the drying is done

Drying and repair are different jobs, and both matter. A house that is dry but still has torn-out drywall is not finished, and a house that is repaired over wet framing is a mold problem waiting to happen. The order matters, so the written scope lists mitigation first and repair second.

Repair work often includes new drywall, insulation, baseboards, paint, subfloor sections and flooring. Which of those apply depends on what the moisture readings showed. A clean supply line leak caught quickly may only need drying. A creek flood that soaked the carpet pad and the bottom foot of drywall will need removal and replacement.

Ask for mitigation and repair to be itemized separately. That makes it easier to compare quotes, and easier to explain the claim to your insurer. The guide to what an estimate should include gives a checklist you can hold any company to.

Insurance claims

Water damage and your insurance claim

Documentation that helps your claim

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, and most exclude flood. The Indiana Department of Insurance notes that a flood policy normally does not take effect until 30 days after purchase, so the time to ask about it is before the creek rises.

We document what we find with moisture readings, photos and a written scope of work. We do not approve claims or promise payment, and the decision belongs to your insurer. Read the claim steps guide before you call your carrier.

Questions from Guilford homeowners

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when a restoration professional is available for your address, and the dispatcher can tell you what to do while you wait, such as shutting off the main water valve.

How much does water damage restoration cost in Guilford?

The price depends on the water category, the square footage affected, the materials that got wet, the number of drying days and whether a rebuild is needed. Clean supply water in one room is a very different job from a creek flood through a finished lower level. Call and describe the loss, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Indiana?

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage from a rising creek or river is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and see the plain-language guide on coverage.

What should I do first when I find water in my home?

Stop the source if you can reach the shutoff safely, then call the emergency line. Keep people and pets out of dirty water, lift valuables off the floor, and turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry surface. If water touches outlets or a gas appliance, leave and call 911 or the utility first.

Which ZIP codes and towns do you serve?

The main service area is Guilford, ZIP 47022, in York Township. Nearby Dearborn County towns are Lawrenceburg (47025), Aurora (47001), Moores Hill (47032) and West Harrison (47060).

Do you repair the damage or only dry it out?

Both are handled in order. Extraction and drying come first because wet drywall and subfloor cannot be rebuilt over. Once moisture readings reach the dry standard, the repair work follows, from replacing drywall and baseboards to flooring. Each step is written into the scope you see before work starts.

Is a creek flood on Tanners Creek handled differently from a pipe leak?

Yes. Creek water is treated as contaminated, which means more material is removed and more cleaning is done before drying starts. A clean supply line leak can often be dried in place. The flood damage page explains the difference step by step.
