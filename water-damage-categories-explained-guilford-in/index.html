Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

The category tells you how contaminated the water is. It decides what can be dried, what must be removed and who should handle the cleanup.

Restoration professionals sort water damage into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a sanitary source. Category 2 is gray water with some contamination. Category 3 is grossly contaminated water that can carry disease. The category sets the cleanup plan and the safety steps, and it can get worse as time passes.

Category 1: clean water

Water from a broken supply line, a faucet or a failed water heater starts out clean. Porous materials can often be dried in place when the water is caught quickly. A clean source is not always clean for long. If it sits on dirty floors or soaks into wood for a day or more, the category can shift.

Category 2: gray water

Gray water carries some contamination that could make people ill. Typical sources are a dishwasher or washer overflow, a sump pump failure or water that has passed through insulation or soiled surfaces. Carpet pad is usually removed, and surfaces are cleaned and disinfected.

Category 3: black water

Sewage, river or creek flood water, and standing water that has been sitting for days are in this group. Porous materials such as carpet, pad and soft drywall are removed. Do not touch it, and keep children and pets away. See sewage backup cleanup and flood damage restoration.

Why the category can change

Time and contact move water down the scale. Clean water left on a carpet for days grows bacteria. Rain that runs through a roof and insulation can be gray by the time it reaches the ceiling. For that reason, fast action is not only about convenience. It keeps the water in a lower category and saves materials.

The EPA advises that absorbent or porous materials such as ceiling tiles and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy, and that plumbing leaks and other water problems should be fixed as soon as possible. That is the practical reason the categories matter.

Guilford water categories: local facts

Guilford sits at the forks of Tanners Creek. Creek water that enters a home from outside is treated as Category 3, because it picks up soil, runoff and debris on the way. Water from a broken indoor supply line in the same house starts as Category 1.

The Guilford Covered Bridge Park, where the 1879 bridge stands at State Road 1 and York Ridge, is a handy landmark when you describe your location on a call. The dispatcher will ask about the water source in the same call.

Sources: Wikipedia entry for Guilford, Indiana. EPA, Mold Cleanup in Your Home, epa.gov/mold.

What the categories mean for your family

Categories also tell you how careful to be. Wear gloves and boots around Category 2 and 3 water, wash hands afterward and keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away. Do not run the HVAC system while contaminated water is present, because it can spread particles through the house. Open windows only if the weather allows.

If you are unsure, treat the water as one category worse than it looks. Professionals can test, measure and decide what is safe to dry in place.

Common examples, sorted

  • A pipe splits in a wall and runs for an hour: Category 1.
  • A dishwasher overflows onto a kitchen floor: Category 2.
  • A sump pump fails and a basement takes on groundwater: Category 2 or 3.
  • Tanners Creek rises into a lower level: Category 3.
  • A toilet overflow with waste: Category 3.

The same room can have more than one category if several sources are involved. The professional sets the plan by the dirtiest source.

Ask the crew to note the category in the scope, since it affects what the insurer expects to see.

A category is a starting point, not a final label. Callers in Aurora, Lawrenceburg and nearby towns often ask the same question, and the answer starts with the source of the water.

Common questions

Is water from a washing machine Category 1 or 2?

Usually Category 2, because it carries detergent and soil. A broken supply line to the machine is Category 1.

Can Category 3 carpet be saved?

Generally no. Porous materials in black water are removed and replaced.

How long before clean water turns gray?

It depends on what it touches, but a day or two on dirty or porous surfaces is enough to change the plan.

Do I need a professional for Category 1 water?

Often yes for anything beyond a small spill, because hidden moisture needs readings.
