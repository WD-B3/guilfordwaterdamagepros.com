Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

Water damage calls happen under pressure. A short list of questions helps you compare companies and avoid vague scopes.

By the teamGuilford, IN

Most people hire a water damage company during an emergency, which is when they are least able to compare. A few questions, asked in two minutes, separate a careful operation from a vague one. You do not need technical knowledge. You need clear answers in plain language.

About the company

  1. Are you licensed and insured, and can you send proof? Ask for the insurance certificate.
  2. Who will be at my house, and are they employees or subcontractors?
  3. Is the emergency line answered by a person, and when can someone be there?

Be careful with any promise of an exact time of arrival. Storms put many people in the same situation, and an honest company says same-day response is possible when a pro is free near you. We say it that way because it is accurate.

About the work

  1. What exactly will you do on day one, and what happens after?
  2. How will you measure that the structure is dry? Ask about moisture readings and a dry standard.
  3. What equipment will you place, and how many days do you expect?
  4. Which materials do you plan to remove, and why? Ask to see readings that justify cutting.

About price and paperwork

Ask for a written scope and price before work starts. The Indiana Department of Insurance tells homeowners to determine repair costs before meeting the adjuster and to ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer. The same itemized approach works with a contractor. Ask what is included, what is excluded and what could change the price.

Red flags to notice

  • Pressure to sign a large contract on the spot.
  • No written scope or only a lump-sum price.
  • Promises about what your insurer will pay.
  • No moisture readings or monitoring plan.
  • Demolition of walls or floors with no explanation.

What a first visit should look like

On arrival, a careful crew finds and stops the source if possible, then walks the whole affected area with a moisture meter before touching anything. They should explain what they see in plain language, show you the readings, and describe the plan for extraction, removal and drying. You should get a chance to ask questions before work starts.

Expect photographs, a written scope and a request for your insurance details if you plan to file a claim. Be wary of anyone who starts cutting walls within minutes, or who cannot show readings that justify it.

After the first day, ask when someone will return to check readings and move equipment. Drying is a process, not an event, and a plan with dates is a good sign.

What a fair answer sounds like on the phone

A fair call opens with questions about safety and the source of the water, then explains what happens next. It avoids hard sell language and avoids promises about what the insurer will pay. It gives an honest window for response and tells you what to do while you wait. If you hear pressure instead, hang up and call another company.

Keep the answers in writing

After you talk, send a short text or email that repeats what you were told about scope, equipment, readings and price. A company that is comfortable with its answers will confirm them. Save the reply. If a dispute comes up later, the written record settles it faster than memory.

Do this even in an emergency. It takes two minutes and prevents most misunderstandings about what was agreed.

Common questions

Should I get more than one quote?

If the situation allows. Water damage is time sensitive, so ask each company for a written scope you can compare.

Is a company with the lowest price the best choice?

Not necessarily. Compare the scope, equipment and monitoring, since a cheap quote may leave out drying inside walls.

Can a company promise to be there in an hour?

Be skeptical of any promise of an exact arrival time. We aim for same-day response when a crew has an opening for your address.

Do I have to use the company my insurer suggests?

Ask your insurer about your options. You can compare scopes before you decide.
