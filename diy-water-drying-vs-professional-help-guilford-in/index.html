DIY Water Drying or Professional Help in Guilford, IN?

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

A small clean spill is a home job. Anything bigger, dirtier or hidden in a wall needs readings and equipment.

By the teamGuilford, IN

Some water problems are a mop and a box fan. Others are not, and the difference is easy to miss. The deciding factors are the water source, how much got wet, how long it has been wet and what is under the surface. This guide helps you sort which side of the line your problem sits on.

What you can handle yourself

  • A small amount of clean water, such as a spilled bucket, caught within an hour.
  • A wet hard floor with no seams, cabinets or baseboards involved.
  • A damp towel or rug that can be washed and dried outside.
  • A minor drip that has been stopped and affects a small, visible area.

For these cases, remove standing water, blot or wipe the area, open windows or run fans, and run a dehumidifier. Check the area again the next day to be sure it feels dry. If the smell does not go away, a hidden area may still be damp.

When it needs professional help

  • The water came from sewage, a drain backup, a creek or a long-standing source.
  • Water soaked carpet pad, drywall, insulation or subfloor.
  • More than one room is wet, or a ceiling has stained or sagged.
  • Water has been there for more than a day.
  • You see mold, or the wet area covers a large surface.

The EPA says moldy areas smaller than about 10 square feet can usually be handled by the homeowner, and that larger problems should follow professional guidance. The 10 square foot figure is a handy rough limit for any water problem: if it is bigger, get a reading.

Guilford DIY facts

Guilford sits at the forks of Tanners Creek in York Township. Creek water that enters a home is outside water and is not a DIY job, because it can carry contamination and soaks into materials.

The EPA says to fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible and dry all items completely. It says absorbent or porous materials such as ceiling tiles and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

Sources: Wikipedia entry for Guilford, Indiana. EPA, Mold Cleanup in Your Home, epa.gov/mold.

Why equipment matters

Fans move air but do not remove moisture from a closed room. Dehumidifiers pull water out of the air, and professionals add air movers aimed at walls and floors, plus meters that show when drying is finished. Without readings, a home can feel dry while framing is still wet. See structural drying and dehumidification.

How to dry a small area safely

  1. Remove standing water with towels, a mop or a wet-dry vacuum.
  2. Lift furniture off wet carpet, using foil or plastic blocks under the legs.
  3. Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower, and run a dehumidifier in closed rooms.
  4. Point fans across the surface, not into a closed wall.
  5. Check again after 24 hours with your hand, and remove baseboards if you suspect moisture behind them.

If anything still feels cool or damp after a day or two, stop and call for a moisture check. A small problem you dry yourself is a good outcome. A small problem you cover up is how larger repairs begin.

Take photos along the way. If the problem proves bigger than you thought, the record helps with any claim.

A quick self-check before you decide

  • Can you see the whole wet area and reach every part of it?
  • Was the water clean, and is it gone after wiping?
  • Are walls, cabinets and insulation dry to the touch after a day?
  • Does the room smell normal after drying?

If any answer is no, a moisture reading is the next step.

What professionals bring that a home does not have

Moisture meters, thermal cameras, extraction units, air movers sized for the room and commercial dehumidifiers make the difference between a floor that feels dry and a structure that is dry. They also bring experience in knowing where water hides, such as under cabinet toe-kicks and behind baseboards.

Common questions

Can I dry a wet carpet myself?

A small clean spill, yes, if caught quickly. Larger areas need extraction and a decision about the pad.

Is a box fan enough to dry a wall?

No. Fans move air but do not remove moisture from a closed cavity.

How do I know if my Guilford basement is a DIY job?

If water is more than a thin film, came from outside or has been there a day, call. Outside water carries contamination.

Will a dehumidifier alone fix a damp room?

It helps with air moisture but not with materials that are soaked.
