What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

The water source and the material decide the answer. Hard goods can often be cleaned. Porous goods from dirty water usually cannot.

After a leak or flood, the first question is what to save. Two things decide the answer: how clean the water was and how porous the item is. Clean water on a hard surface comes off easily. Dirty water in a sofa does not. Sort with photos in hand, because your insurer may want to see the items.

Start with the water source

Water from a supply line is far less risky than sewage or creek water. Outside or sewage water contaminates anything it touches, and porous items from that event are usually discarded. The category guide in sewage backup cleanup explains why.

Porous means absorbs

The EPA notes that absorbent or porous materials, such as ceiling tiles and carpet, may have to be thrown away if they become moldy. The same logic applies to anything soft that stayed wet. Fabric, stuffing, particleboard and paper hold water and microbes, and drying them rarely returns them to a safe condition.

Save for the adjuster

The Indiana Department of Insurance advises homeowners to keep damaged personal property for the adjuster to inspect. Photograph each item, take a video inventory of contents and keep a list with the purchase price if you know it. Do not discard large items until you have documented them and the insurer says it is fine.

Special cases

Photographs, family papers and electronics each have special handling, because they cannot be replaced and because some can be rescued. Move them first and handle them gently. Keep them away from heat, which makes things worse. Where flooring is the main concern, hardwood floor water damage repair covers what can be dried.

Kitchen, bath and closet items

  • Pots, pans, glass and ceramic dishes can be washed in hot soapy water and disinfected when the water was dirty.
  • Wooden cutting boards and utensils that soaked in contaminated water are usually discarded.
  • Canned food with no dents can be saved if the cans are washed and disinfected, and everything in cardboard or loose packaging is discarded.
  • Medicines and cosmetics that touched flood or sewage water are discarded.
  • Clothing and linens can often be washed in hot water if the water was clean, and heavily soiled items may not recover.

When in doubt, throw it out is a fair rule for food, medicine and anything a child puts in their mouth. For sentimental objects, set them aside and ask a professional before you decide. Many items can be cleaned more thoroughly than a household wash allows.

A note on children's items

Toys, car seats and bedding deserve extra care. Hard plastic toys can be washed and disinfected, but plush toys and anything with foam that soaked in dirty water should be discarded. Car seats exposed to floodwater are generally replaced because the straps and internal parts cannot be reliably cleaned. Check the manufacturer's guidance.

How to dispose of what you discard

Bag wet soft goods in heavy plastic to keep dirty water from dripping through the house, and carry them out by the shortest route. Large items may need a bulk pickup or a dumpster, so ask your trash provider about the rules. Keep a count and photos for the claim before anything leaves the property.

Common questions

Can a wet sofa be saved?

If the water was clean and the sofa dried fast, sometimes. Contaminated water means the sofa is discarded.

Should I throw things out before the adjuster comes?

Not before documenting them. Keep damaged items the adjuster may want to see, unless they are unsafe.

What about food and medicine that got wet in Guilford?

Discard food and medicine that touched flood or sewage water, and anything in containers that cannot be sealed.

How do I save a wet rug?

Take it outside or to a dry space, extract water and dry it quickly. Rugs from contaminated water are discarded.
