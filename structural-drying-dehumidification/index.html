My walls feel damp and the floor is still wet. What do I do?

A damp wall, a floor that stays cool and a musty odor after the water is gone mean the structure still holds moisture. Surfaces can feel dry while framing and subfloor are wet. The trade process that fixes this is structural drying, which uses moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers to remove water that a pump cannot reach.

Structural drying and dehumidification is the controlled removal of moisture from building materials after a water loss. It is the part of restoration that decides whether you get mold and warped floors or a dry, repairable house. Water mitigation, the broader term for stopping and limiting the damage, includes extraction and drying and is part of every job.

Moisture mapping: finding the water you cannot see

A moisture meter reads how much water is in wood and drywall. A thermal camera shows cool spots where moisture sits. Together they let a technician map the loss, room by room and wall by wall, and mark the wet areas. The map is the baseline that proves progress.

Readings are compared to a dry reference, the moisture level of similar unaffected material in your own home. The goal is to return wet materials to that standard, and the job ends when the numbers say so.

Air movers

High-velocity fans move air across wet surfaces and speed evaporation.

LGR dehumidifiers

Low-grain refrigerant units pull moisture out of the air so evaporation can continue.

Desiccant dehumidifiers

Used in cold or very dry conditions where refrigerant units perform poorly.

Injectidry and panel systems

Pull air through wall cavities and hardwood floors to dry them without removing them.

How a drying job runs

1

Map

Moisture meters and thermal imaging define the wet area and set the baseline.

2

Set equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to move air across wet surfaces and out of the space.

3

Monitor

Technicians return, take readings, move equipment and adjust the setup.

4

Confirm

Final readings show materials at the dry standard, and equipment is removed.

Drying times vary. Drywall and carpet dry sooner than dense hardwood, concrete and framing. A calendar estimate is a guess until readings come in, and good providers adjust as the data arrives. A loss that was clean, caught early and small can finish in a few days, while a larger loss takes longer.

Drying relies on a controlled space. Windows and doors are closed, the heat is kept on and equipment runs continuously. It is loud, and warm. That is normal. Moving equipment or shutting it off early can leave pockets of moisture that support mold.

What to do while equipment is running

  • Do not switch off or unplug dehumidifiers and air movers to save power. Drying stops when they stop.
  • Do not touch equipment cords with wet hands, and keep children and pets away from them.
  • Do not open doors and windows if the crew has set up a closed drying space.
  • Tell the crew if a breaker trips or a smell appears.

Common questions

How do I know my house is actually dry?

Moisture readings compared to a dry reference. A surface that feels dry can still hold water. Ask for the readings in writing.

Why do drying machines run for days?

Wet materials release water slowly. The equipment has to keep removing it, or the moisture goes back into the structure.

Can I just use a household dehumidifier?

For minor dampness, yes. After a real water loss, commercial units move far more water, and fans are needed to move air across wet surfaces.

Do you handle drying emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

The line takes active water around the clock. After the first visit, drying equipment runs continuously until the readings are done.
