Businesses and multi-unit buildings need a restoration plan that limits downtime, protects inventory and documents the loss for the carrier and the landlord.

Commercial water damage restoration is the extraction, drying and repair of a business or multi-unit building after a pipe failure, roof leak, sprinkler release or flood. In Guilford and the nearby Dearborn County towns, losses range from a leak in a small shop to a failure that crosses units. If water is spreading in a building, call the 24/7 emergency line and, when it is safe, shut off the water at the main or the zone valve.

Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor first, if it is safe. Cut power to the affected area only if the panel is dry. Notify the property manager or landlord, because lease terms may assign responsibility for repairs.

When a business water loss is an emergency

Every hour a business is closed costs money. Water also spreads through shared walls, ceilings and floors into neighboring spaces. That makes speed worth more here than at home. After-hours and weekend calls are common, because many failures happen when the building is empty, and same-day response is offered to the extent a crew can reach your building.

A fast start helps in other ways. Documentation made during the first visit helps the carrier and the landlord agree on the scope. Photos, moisture readings and an inventory of affected property make claims easier to explain.

Retail and restaurants

Floors, shelving, stock and kitchens. Food and health rules may require cleaning before reopening.

Offices and shared buildings

Ceilings, carpet tile, drywall and electronics. Leaks can pass between tenants.

Multi-unit residential

Water from one unit can affect those above and below. Coordination with owners and tenants is part of the job.

Storage and light industrial

Concrete floors, racking and inventory. Pallets and boxed goods wick water.

How a commercial job differs from a home job

Larger floor area means more equipment, larger extraction units and more coordination. Business hours drive scheduling, so work may be done in phases, after hours or on weekends to limit disruption. Commercial buildings also often have multiple stakeholders, such as owner, tenant and insurer, each with different needs.

The same standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, applies whether the building is a home or a business. Ask any company whether its crews work to it, and ask for moisture logs. Contaminated water, such as sewage, follows the steps on the sewage backup cleanup page.

What to do first, and what not to do

  • Keep staff and customers out of wet areas, and mark slippery floors.
  • Do not touch wet electrical equipment, and switch off power to the affected area from a dry panel.
  • Do not use wet vacuums or fans plugged into wet circuits.
  • Photograph inventory and equipment before anything is moved.

Common questions

Do you handle commercial water emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and business failures often happen after closing. We provide same-day response where a free crew can get there.

Can you work around business hours?

Often, yes. Phased work, after-hours drying and zone-by-zone plans can keep part of a business open. Describe your hours when you call.

Who pays, the tenant or the landlord?

It depends on the lease and the cause. Check the lease, notify the landlord and your insurer, and keep records of everything.

What documentation do you provide?

Moisture readings, photos and a written scope of work. They support claims, but approval is decided by the carrier.
