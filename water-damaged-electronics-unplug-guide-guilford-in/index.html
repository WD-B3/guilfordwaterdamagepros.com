Water-Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

Safety first. Never touch plugged-in electronics or outlets in standing water. Once the power is safely off, wet devices can sometimes be saved.

Water and electricity are a bad combination, and the danger is greater than the damage to the device. The rule is simple: if water is on the floor and electronics are plugged in, do not walk in and unplug them. Turn the power off at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

Safety steps, in order

  1. Stay out of rooms where water covers outlets, cords or power strips.
  2. If the panel is in a dry spot, switch off the breakers for the wet area.
  3. If you cannot reach the panel safely, call the utility or an electrician.
  4. Do not use extension cords through wet areas.
  5. Have an electrician check wet outlets, panels and appliances before you use them.

A flooded basement with a furnace, water heater or panel is a special case. Do not enter. See basement water damage cleanup for how those jobs are handled once power is made safe.

What to do with wet devices

  • Unplug only after the area is safe and your hands are dry.
  • Do not turn on a wet device to test it.
  • Remove batteries and external cards from phones, cameras and remotes.
  • Wipe off clean water and dry with air movement, not heat.
  • If the water was dirty, do not try to clean boards yourself. A repair shop can.

Why speed matters

Corrosion starts quickly on circuit boards, especially with dirty water. Devices that are dried and opened within a day or two have a better chance than ones left wet. Drying a room also lowers humidity, which protects electronics that were not soaked. See structural drying and dehumidification for the room itself.

Appliances and built-in equipment

Furnaces, water heaters, washers, dryers and refrigerators all have electrical components that can be damaged by flooding. If water reached the motor, control board or wiring, do not run the appliance until a qualified technician has checked it. Gas appliances need a gas technician as well, because water can damage controls and valves.

Heat pumps and outdoor AC units that sat in water can fail later, not right away. Corrosion can show up weeks after the flood. Keep your receipts and the technician's report, since they help a claim.

Whole-home signs to watch

  • Breakers that trip repeatedly after drying.
  • Outlets or switches that feel warm, smell burnt or show discoloration.
  • Lights that flicker in rooms that were wet.
  • Appliances that hum, smell or shut off unexpectedly.

Any of these after a water event deserves an electrician's visit. Do not wait for a small problem to become a fire risk.

Documenting electronics for a claim

Take photos of each damaged device, including the model and serial number if visible. Keep the devices until the adjuster has seen them or approved disposal. Receipts, credit card statements and photos of the item in use can help show value. A written technician's note that a device is not repairable is a strong record.

Surge protectors and batteries

Power strips and surge protectors that got wet should be thrown away, since internal protection parts fail silently. Lithium batteries that were submerged can swell or overheat later, so do not charge them. Store them outside on a non-flammable surface and take them to a battery drop-off point.

Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms near the flooded area should be tested after the power is restored.

Keep a list of every device that was wet, with the date you tested it and the result. Some failures appear days later, and the list helps you connect a late failure to the water event when you talk to your insurer or a repair shop.

Common questions

Is it safe to unplug a TV standing in an inch of water?

No. Leave the room, switch off power at a dry panel or call the utility or an electrician.

Does rice dry out a wet phone?

Not reliably. Power it off, remove the battery or card if possible and take it to a repair shop.

Can I use outlets that got wet?

Not until an electrician has checked them.

Will insurance cover ruined electronics in Guilford?

Often for covered sudden losses, but flood is usually excluded. Ask your agent and document everything.
