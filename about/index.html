Guilford Water Damage Pros answers water damage calls for Guilford, Indiana and nearby towns. The work covers water extraction, structural drying, cleanup of contaminated water and the repairs that follow. Every page on this site is written by the team, and none of it is a substitute for reading your own insurance policy.

How the service works

There is no front desk and no form. You call the emergency line, describe the water and get a plain answer about what happens next. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead. We send someone the same day when a crew is free to come to you.

What we do and do not do

  • We remove water, dry structures, clean affected areas and repair drywall, flooring and finishes.
  • We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your insurer.
  • We do not decide coverage. Your policy and your insurer do.
  • We do not offer mold remediation, which is a separate trade.

Proof of insurance and licensing

This site is a call line for water damage in Dearborn County, and it does not claim any license or certification on behalf of a contractor. Ask whoever comes to your home for proof of insurance and any license your town requires before work begins. It is a basic question to ask any contractor.

Where we work

The main area is Guilford in York Township, Dearborn County. Nearby we serve Lawrenceburg, Aurora, Moores Hill and West Harrison. The office address is 682 W Eads Pkwy, Lawrenceburg, IN 47025. See the service area page for distances and ZIP codes.

What we write about

The service pages explain each type of water damage in plain language, with local facts only where we could source them. We do not publish reviews or ratings on this site, because we do not have verified ones to show. If that changes, we will add only real ones.

How a typical job unfolds

Most jobs begin with a phone call and end with a dry, repaired room. In between, the sequence is steady. First the source is stopped and standing water is removed. Then the crew takes moisture readings, places drying equipment and returns to check progress. When readings reach the dry standard, repairs begin or the scope is handed to your insurer.

Along the way you will hear plain explanations of what is wet, what can be saved and what has to be removed. You can ask to see readings at any visit. Decisions about walls, floors and contents are yours, and each one comes with a reason.

What we record on every job

  • Photographs of the damage before work starts and after drying ends.
  • Moisture readings on arrival and at every return visit.
  • The type and number of drying units placed and the days they ran.
  • A list of materials removed, with the reason for each.
  • The written scope and price you agreed to before work began.

This record helps your insurer and protects you if a question comes up later. You can ask for a copy at the end of the job.

How the pages on this site are written

Each page is written to answer a real question a homeowner has during or after water damage. Local facts, such as the creek forks at Guilford or the elevation of Moores Hill, come from public sources like Wikipedia entries and the Indiana Department of Insurance. Where we could not find a source for a local claim, we left it out.

What we ask of you

Water damage goes better when the first call is clear. Tell us what you see, even if you are not sure what it means. Shut off the water if you can do so safely, and keep people away from hazards. Take photographs before you move things. Ask any question, however small, because a good answer early prevents mistakes later.

If you have insurance, call your agent or the claims line soon after you call us. The Indiana Department of Insurance advises reporting a claim as soon as possible because a policy might set a deadline. We can supply records, but the claim itself is between you and your insurer.

Commercial and rental properties

Businesses, landlords and property managers use the same line. Commercial jobs are planned around opening hours, tenants and safety rules, and the scope names who approved each stage. Rental owners should tell us who holds the policy and who the contact is on site.
