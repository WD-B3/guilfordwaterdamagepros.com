What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

A useful estimate is specific. It names rooms, tasks, equipment, days and what is not included, so you can compare it with another one or with the adjuster's.

An estimate is a promise about what will be done. A vague one leaves room for surprise charges and arguments with your insurer. A specific one shows you what the company will do, in what order and for what price. Use this list to read any water damage estimate, ours or someone else's.

The ten items a good estimate lists

  1. The property address and the date of the loss.
  2. The source and category of the water, as the company understands it.
  3. A room-by-room scope that names each area and the work planned.
  4. Extraction method and expected time.
  5. Drying equipment by type and quantity, and the expected number of days.
  6. Moisture monitoring, with the dry standard and who records the readings.
  7. Materials to be removed, with the reason for each.
  8. Cleaning and disinfection steps, if any.
  9. Repairs and rebuild, either priced or listed as a separate scope.
  10. Exclusions, change-order terms, and payment terms.

That list may look long, but most items are one line each. If an estimate is a single lump sum with no scope, ask for a breakdown.

How it fits with your claim

The Indiana Department of Insurance advises homeowners to work out repair costs before meeting the adjuster and to ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer. An itemized estimate from a restoration company is a direct match. If the adjuster's numbers differ, compare line by line rather than total to total.

Do not sign away your right to choose a contractor or assign your claim to a company without reading what it means. Ask your insurer if you are unsure.

Guilford estimate facts

Guilford, ZIP 47022, is in York Township, Dearborn County. Your estimate should list the full property address with the ZIP, since insurers match documents to the policy address.

The Indiana Department of Insurance suggests asking the claims adjuster for an itemized explanation of the settlement offer and advises against making permanent repairs before the insurer's inspection. A good estimate separates drying work from permanent repair so the sequence is clear.

Sources: Indiana Department of Insurance, Insurance Claim Tips, in.gov/idoi/consumer-services/insurance-claim-tips.

Common surprises, and how to avoid them

  • Added equipment days: ask what the plan is if readings are still high.
  • Hidden damage: ask how the estimate changes if walls are opened.
  • Disposal and contents: ask whether they are included.
  • Permits or specialty trades: ask whether any are needed.

A sample scope, in plain words

Here is the kind of wording that works. Living room, 14 by 16 feet: extract standing water, remove and dispose of carpet pad, lift carpet and dry, set four air movers and one dehumidifier for three days, record moisture readings each day, treat the subfloor surface. Hallway wall, lower four feet: remove baseboard, cut drywall at 24 inches and dry the cavity.

Notice what is there. Rooms have sizes, actions have names, equipment has counts and drying has a length. An estimate with that detail lets you compare companies and lets an adjuster see exactly what was done.

Notice what is missing too. Prices for repairs that depend on the final readings can be listed as a range or a second phase, with an agreement to confirm the price after drying.

When to ask for a revised estimate

Ask for a revision if the scope changes, if readings stay high after the planned days, or if hidden damage is found. A revision should say what changed and why, and it should be approved before the extra work begins. Keep each version with its date. A paper trail of estimates helps if a question arises later.

Read the fine print on payment

Look for terms about deposits, progress payments and what happens if your insurer pays late. Ask whether the company bills the insurer directly or expects payment from you first. A clear payment section avoids stress at the end of the job, when you most want it to be simple.

Also check how long the estimate stays valid and whether material prices can change. If the repair phase will start weeks after drying, ask for the price to be confirmed in writing before that phase begins.

Keep the signed copy where you can find it, and send a photo of it to yourself. Paper gets wet in the same events that make people need estimates.

Common questions

Is a verbal estimate enough?

No. Ask for a written scope and price before work starts.

Should an estimate include repairs?

It can, but drying and repairs are often listed separately so you can see each.

What if the estimate changes after walls are opened?

A good estimate says how changes are handled and asks for approval first.

How do I compare my Guilford estimate with the adjuster's?

Line by line, room by room, using the itemized lists from each.
