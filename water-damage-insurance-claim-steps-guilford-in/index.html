Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Guilford, IN Homes

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

The order matters. Document the damage, make temporary repairs, report the loss and hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has looked.

A water damage claim goes better when the record is strong from the first hour. The Indiana Department of Insurance gives homeowners a short order of operations, and this page puts it in plain steps for a Guilford house. None of this replaces your policy, so read it and call your agent.

Step 1: Stop the water and stay safe

Shut the main valve or the fixture valve. If water is near the panel or outlets, do not touch them and call the utility. Keep people and pets out of dirty water. Safety comes before photos.

Step 2: Photograph and film before you touch anything

The state's claim tips say to take photographs or video of the damage before making temporary repairs. Shoot wide views of each room, then close-ups of the waterline, the source and ruined items. A walk-through video with narration works well. Add a quick shot of the water meter or the failed part.

Step 3: Protect the property

The same guidance says to make temporary repairs that protect the property from further damage. Extraction and drying are part of that, and so are tarps and board-ups. See water extraction for what happens first. Keep every receipt.

1

Report the loss

Call your agent or the claims hotline as soon as possible. The state notes that your policy might require notice within a set time.

2

Save damaged items

Keep damaged personal property for the adjuster to inspect, unless it is unsafe.

3

Hold permanent repairs

The state advises against permanent repairs before the insurer's inspection.

4

Prepare your numbers

Work out what repairs will cost before you meet the adjuster, and ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer.

Step 4: Build a home inventory

The state suggests a video inventory of property and contents. Even a rough list of the room, the item, the age and the price helps. For soft goods, see the sorting notes on carpet water damage restoration, where pad and rugs get the most questions.

What a restoration company adds to the file

A good drying record includes the date of arrival, moisture readings on day one and each visit, a list of equipment placed and the readings at the finish. Ask for a copy. Adjusters often want it, and it protects you if a question comes up later about why walls were opened.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and we do not make coverage decisions. If your insurer and the scope disagree, ask the adjuster to name what they would change and why.

Working with the adjuster

The adjuster inspects, measures and writes an estimate. Be present if you can, walk through each room and point out every affected area, including ones that look minor. Bring your photos, your written scope and your list of contents. Ask when to expect the estimate and whether a second visit is needed.

If the adjuster misses something, say so in writing and send photos. Keep a log of every call with the date, the name and what was said. A short, factual record is more persuasive than a long complaint, and it keeps the claim moving.

A simple claim timeline

On day one, stop the water, photograph, protect the property and report the loss. In the first week, meet the adjuster, share your scope and keep drying records. After the drying finishes, repairs are planned with the insurer's approval. Keeping a dated log for each stage makes it easy to answer questions later.

Common questions

Should I clean up before the adjuster arrives?

Protect the property and dry it, but photograph first and save damaged items. Hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected.

How soon do I need to report a water loss?

As soon as possible. Your policy may set a deadline, so read the conditions section and call your agent.

Can I keep the receipts for emergency drying?

Yes, and you should. Receipts for temporary repairs and protective work are part of the claim record.

What should I do if the settlement offer looks low?

Ask for an itemized explanation of how the number was reached and compare it to your written scope.
