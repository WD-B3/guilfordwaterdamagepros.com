Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Guilford, IN

A slab leak is a supply line failure under or inside a concrete floor. It is hard to see, which is why the clues matter and why drying matters as much as the plumbing repair.

My floor is warm or my water bill jumped. What is going on?

A warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when nothing is on, or a water bill that rose with no explanation can point to a leaking supply line under a concrete slab. Water in these lines is under constant pressure, so a pinhole can run for weeks before anything shows. The trade name is slab leak water damage, and it is both a plumbing and a drying problem.

To check, shut off every fixture, then look at the water meter. If the leak indicator or dial moves, water is escaping somewhere. Closing the main valve stops the loss while you arrange a plumber and a restoration visit.

Slab leak water damage is the moisture, flooring and wall damage left when a pressurized supply line under or within a concrete slab leaks into the building. It only applies to homes built on a concrete slab. If your home has a basement, similar symptoms usually point to a different source, covered under basement water damage.

When a slab leak is an emergency

A confirmed leak under the floor is an urgent call, especially when water is surfacing, flooring is lifting or a warm spot is growing. The 24/7 emergency line takes these calls at any hour, and help the same day is offered when your address is within reach of an open crew. Close the main valve first.

  1. Shut off the main water valve.
  2. Check the meter with every fixture off to confirm a leak.
  3. Move furniture and valuables off any wet flooring.
  4. Photograph wet spots, lifted flooring and the meter reading.
  5. Call the emergency line and a plumber who locates leaks.

What a slab leak does to a house

Water under a slab moves sideways and up through cracks and joints. It soaks the underlayment, flooring and the bottom of walls, and it can undermine the soil under the slab. Tile can crack, hardwood can cup and carpet can stay damp underneath. The slow nature of the leak means the area is often wetter than it looks.

Once the plumber finds the leak, a restoration crew checks moisture around the area. That includes baseboards, drywall, flooring and sometimes walls in adjacent rooms. The scope depends on how long the leak ran and which materials are involved.

Flooring

Tile, vinyl, laminate and wood can all be affected. Wood and laminate are the most vulnerable.

Baseboards and drywall

Water wicks up from the floor. The bottom of walls often shows staining.

Subfloor and slab

The slab itself can hold moisture. Dry-down time is longer than for framed floors.

Soil under the slab

A long leak can wash out soil. A plumber or engineer checks this.

How the cleanup works

After the plumber stops the leak, the restoration work begins. The crew removes wet flooring that cannot be saved, places air movers and dehumidifiers, and monitors moisture daily. Drying a slab takes longer than drying a wall, because concrete holds water. See structural drying for how that works.

Flooring decisions follow the moisture readings. If hardwood has cupped, it may flatten as it dries, or it may need replacement. If carpet stayed wet, the pad and sometimes the carpet are replaced.

What to do first, and what not to do

  • Close the main water valve if you suspect a leak under the floor.
  • Do not touch a floor that is hot or wet if outlets or cords are nearby. A hot spot can mean a hot water line is leaking.
  • Do not cut into the slab yourself. A plumber should locate the leak first.
  • Do not keep using hot water if the water heater is working harder than usual. Turn it off and call.

Common questions

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water, a rising water bill and a moving meter with all fixtures off are the usual clues. A plumber with leak-detection equipment can confirm.

Why would my floor feel damp but not look wet?

Moisture can rise through concrete slowly. If a floor feels cool and damp, a moisture meter can tell whether it is a leak or condensation.

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Close the main valve and call. Same-day response is offered when a professional is available.

Is a slab leak covered by insurance?

Coverage varies. Water damage from a sudden leak is often covered, while the cost of tearing into the slab may be limited. Ask your insurer and read the policy.
