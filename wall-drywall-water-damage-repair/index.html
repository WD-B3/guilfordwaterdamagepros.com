Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Guilford, IN

Drywall wicks water upward and hides it inside the cavity. Deciding between drying in place and cutting it out is the key choice in wall water damage repair.

Wall and drywall water damage repair is the drying, cutting and replacement of drywall, baseboards, insulation and paint after water reaches a wall. In Guilford the usual causes are leaks inside the wall, water from a floor above and flooding at floor level.

Drywall is paper-faced gypsum that wicks water up from the floor and holds it, so a small stain on the surface can mean a much wetter cavity. Call the 24/7 line if a wall is soft, stained or bubbling.

Do not paint over a wet wall, and do not press hard on a soft area. Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and photograph it, so progress can be tracked.

How water behaves in a wall

Water that enters a wall cavity runs down the framing and soaks the insulation and bottom plate. Drywall wicks it upward, sometimes a foot or more above the water line, which is why water damage on baseboards often means more wetness behind the wall than the eye sees. Paint bubbles, baseboard swelling and soft drywall are the surface clues.

Wall damage often comes from another source. A leak from a ceiling above, a failed supply line or flooding at floor level all end here. The repair starts by tracing the water to its source.

Baseboard swelling

Wood and MDF baseboards swell and separate from the wall. A sign of wicking.

Bubbling or peeling paint

Water between paint and drywall pushes it away. Often a long, slow leak.

Soft drywall

Drywall that gives under thumb pressure has lost strength.

Stains that grow

A stain that spreads means an active source.

Dry in place or cut it out

Clean water caught quickly can often be dried inside the wall. Small holes are drilled at the base and a dehumidifier pushes dry air through the cavity, which saves the drywall. If the drywall has softened or the water was contaminated, a flood cut is made, which is a straight cut about 12 to 24 inches above the waterline. The wet drywall and insulation come out, and the cavity dries open.

Finishing: paint, texture and the ghost stain

A finished repair needs more than patching. New drywall is taped, mudded and sanded, then textured to match. Stain-blocking primer keeps old stains from bleeding through paint. Matching the wall color usually means painting the full wall so the patch does not show.

If a stain returns after paint, the wall is still wet or the stain was not blocked. A moisture meter reading before repainting is the cheapest way to avoid repeating the job.

What to do first, and what not to do

  • Do not drill or cut into a wet wall that may hold wiring. Switch off the circuit first.
  • Do not touch outlets or switches on a wet wall.
  • Do not paint over a damp wall. It hides the signs and traps moisture.
  • Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and photograph it.

Common questions

Can wet drywall be dried instead of replaced?

Sometimes. Clean water caught within a day or two can be dried in place. Soft, stained or contaminated drywall is usually cut out.

Why is my baseboard swollen?

Water wicked up from the floor or came down from the wall. Check the wall behind it for moisture.

Do you handle wall water emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Water still moving in a wall is urgent day or night. A stain with no more moisture can wait for a scheduled check.

Will paint hide a water stain?

Only with stain-blocking primer on dry drywall. Regular paint lets the stain bleed back.
