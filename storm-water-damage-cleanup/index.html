Emergency Storm Water Damage Cleanup in Guilford, IN

Wind-driven rain, torn shingles and fallen limbs let water through the building envelope. The cleanup deals with the interior, and a tarp or roofer deals with the opening.

Storm water damage cleanup is the removal and drying of water that entered a building through wind, rain and storm damage, as opposed to rising creek or river water. In Guilford, a single storm can bring shingle loss, a limb through the roof, water around windows and ceilings that bulge hours later. If water is coming in, protect yourself first and call the 24/7 emergency line.

Stay clear of downed lines, avoid rooms with sagging ceilings and do not climb on a wet roof. If a limb has opened the roof, a temporary tarp from a roofer or a restoration team limits more water while repairs are scheduled.

What to do when storm water is still coming in

The worst water often arrives after the storm itself. Wet insulation holds water against the ceiling until it gives out, and wind-driven rain keeps finding the same gaps. After-hours calls are normal during and after storms, and same-day response is offered subject to crew availability at your address, though severe weather can mean many calls at once.

  • Move furniture and valuables out from under dripping areas and photograph the damage first.
  • Put buckets under active drips and keep people out from under bulging ceilings.
  • Stay out of rooms with water near outlets, and leave if you smell gas.
  • Ask a roofer about a temporary tarp, because repairs on an open roof will fail.

Storm water versus flood water

The difference matters for the cleanup and for insurance. Rain that enters through a damaged roof or window is storm water. Water that rises from a creek, river or runoff and comes in at ground level is flood water, covered under flood damage cleanup. Rain that has run through insulation is often Category 2 by the time it reaches the ceiling.

Policies treat them differently. Wind and rain damage to a covered roof is often covered under a homeowners policy, while flood is usually excluded. The Indiana Department of Insurance advises homeowners to document damage and review the policy for what is covered. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Heavy rain in Dearborn County

Heavy rain does not need a failed roof to cause damage. Long rain saturates soil, overwhelms gutters and pushes water against foundations, and Dearborn County storms can drop a lot of rain in a short time.

The terrain is hilly, so runoff moves quickly toward low points, including creek beds like Tanners Creek at Guilford. After a heavy rain, check the attic, the lower level and the crawl space. See crawl space water damage for below-floor effects.

How storm cleanup runs

1

Protect

Temporary covers go on openings. Water is extracted from the interior.

2

Assess

Moisture meters and thermal cameras trace water through ceilings, walls and insulation.

3

Dry

Wet insulation and damaged drywall are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest.

4

Repair

Interior finishes are replaced once the structure is dry. Roof repair is a roofer's work.

Common questions

Do you handle storm water emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Storms often mean many calls, and a crew is sent the same day when one is free and your address is reachable.

What is the difference between storm and flood damage?

Storm damage is water that comes in through the roof, windows or walls. Flood damage is rising water from outside. They are treated differently for cleanup and often for insurance.

Should I tarp my own roof?

Not if it means climbing a wet roof. A roofer or restoration team can place a temporary cover safely.

How soon should I call after a storm?

As soon as water is inside. Wet insulation and drywall worsen quickly.
