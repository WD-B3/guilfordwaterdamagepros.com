Hidden Valley is a private lake community in eastern Dearborn County, about 4 miles east of Guilford in a straight line. It is a Census-designated place, not a town, and it sits between Greendale to the south and the Ohio state line to the east. If water is spreading through a home here, call the emergency line first. Work usually begins with water extraction, then moves to structural drying.

What Hidden Valley is

The 2020 Census counted 5,529 people and 2,059 housing units in Hidden Valley, with 4.3 percent of those units vacant. The area is about 4.4 square miles and is split between Miller and Lawrenceburg townships. Addresses use ZIP code 47025, which Hidden Valley shares with Lawrenceburg and Greendale. A shared ZIP code does not make it part of either city, and Hidden Valley is neither.

Eagle Country Online reported in May 2026 that the community began in 1969, when developer James J. Rupel of Dayton, Ohio bought more than 1,200 acres along State Line Road. A 150-foot earthen dam was built between 1971 and 1973, and the 150-acre lake reached full pool in 1974. The same report put the community at about 1,800 homes and nearly 5,000 residents, and said the Hidden Valley Lake Property Owners Association, formed in 1972, oversees much of the infrastructure.

Who to call first when a line fails

According to that report, the community runs its own utility systems and maintenance departments. If the water is coming from the street, a failed main or a meter, ask the Property Owners Association who maintains the line on your street before assuming a county or city office does. If the water is coming from inside your home, close the fixture valve or the main valve first, then call the emergency line.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the home's main valve. If it will not turn, say so on the call.

Stay out of standing water near power

If water reaches outlets, cords or the panel, leave the area and call the utility or 911. Do not touch breakers while standing in water.

Photograph before you move things

Take clear photos of the damage and the source when it is safe, then lift small items and rugs out of the wet area.

Keep people and pets away from dirty water

Water from a drain, toilet or the outdoors can carry contamination. Do not clean it up with fans or a household vacuum.

Flood maps and Hidden Valley

On November 9, 2026 we checked FEMA's current National Flood Hazard Layer by sampling about 3,300 points inside the Census boundary. About 99.7 percent fell in Zone X, FEMA's area of minimal flood hazard. A very small share near stream channels fell in mapped Zone A or AE. This is a rough area check and not a finding for any address. Zone X does not mean flooding cannot happen, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for individual streets.

We found no documented flood event for Hidden Valley, so we do not describe it as flood-prone. Water damage in a lake community does not need a flood. Supply lines, water heaters, appliance hoses, roof leaks and drain backups are ordinary sources. If outside water is involved, see flood damage restoration.

Plumbing and appliances

A failed supply line, dishwasher hose or water heater can soak a floor fast. See water heater leak cleanup and appliance leak water damage.

Roof and ceiling leaks

Water that enters at the roof often shows up as a ceiling stain far from the leak. See ceiling water damage repair.

Townhomes and condominiums

Real estate listings show attached homes in Hidden Valley. Water can travel between units, so tell us if your home shares walls or floors.

Empty or seasonal homes

A leak in a home nobody visits can run for days. Check vacant properties after storms and cold snaps.

How the work goes

Extraction removes standing water first. Drying equipment and moisture readings then show whether walls, subfloors and insulation have dried to a normal level. Materials that stay wet, especially after dirty water, are removed and replaced rather than dried in place. Not every material can be saved, and a drying plan is built from readings and not from guesses.

Insurance and records

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the wording of your policy. Sudden plumbing failures are often treated differently from flood or long-term seepage, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage and the work. We do not decide coverage or promise that a claim will be approved. Common questions

Is Hidden Valley part of Guilford?

No. Hidden Valley is a separate community about 4 miles east of Guilford, in Miller and Lawrenceburg townships. It is covered by the same phone line.

What ZIP code does Hidden Valley use?

47025, the same ZIP code used by Lawrenceburg and Greendale. A ZIP code is a mail route and does not show which city or township a home is in.

Is Hidden Valley in a flood zone?

Our area-level check of FEMA's current maps found almost all of it in Zone X, minimal flood hazard, with a few small mapped areas near streams. It does not tell you the status of your own address. Use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer.

Who handles a broken water main in the community?

The community runs its own utility systems, according to local reporting. Ask the Hidden Valley Lake Property Owners Association who maintains the line on your street.

Will insurance pay for the cleanup?

That depends on the cause and your policy. Photos and a written scope can help describe the damage, but the decision belongs to your insurer.
