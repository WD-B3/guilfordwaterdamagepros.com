Emergency Basement Water Damage Cleanup in Guilford, IN

Basement water comes from sump pump failures, groundwater, creek flooding and plumbing leaks. The first questions are how deep the water is and whether it is clean.

Basement water damage cleanup is the pump-out, cleaning and drying of a below-grade room after flooding, seepage or a plumbing failure. In Guilford it commonly follows heavy rain along Tanners Creek, a sump pump that fails, or water that finds a way through the foundation. If your basement is flooding, do not go down if water is near outlets or the electrical panel, and call the 24/7 emergency line.

Water that is deep enough to touch the furnace, water heater or panel is a hazard. Call the utility or 911 if water has reached electrical equipment or a gas appliance, then call us.

When basement water is an emergency

Water that is still rising, deeper than a couple of inches or dark and smelly is an emergency. Basements also hold the things most expensive to lose, including mechanical systems, stored items, finished rooms and sometimes an apartment. Calls come in all night during storms, and same-day response is a goal that depends on a free crew.

Do not enter live water

Stay off the stairs if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances.

Check the sump

If the pump is running but water is rising, it may be overwhelmed. If it is not running, the power or the pump may have failed.

Lift what you can reach

Move items from the lowest shelves and floor, working from the stairs.

Photograph the waterline

Take photos before cleanup starts, including the water level on walls.

Why basements flood in Guilford

Guilford sits at the forks of Tanners Creek, so groundwater and creek levels rise after heavy rain. Basements near the creek can see seepage through floors and walls when the water table is high.

Other causes are less dramatic. A sump pump that loses power during a storm, a clogged floor drain, a cracked foundation wall or a failed water heater can all put water on a basement floor. Check the sump pit and the floor drain first.

Finished basements and contents

A finished basement adds drywall, insulation, carpet and furniture that all hold water. Carpet and pad in flooded rooms are usually removed, and drywall is cut above the waterline. Hard floors and concrete can be cleaned and dried. Furniture and stored goods are sorted into what can be cleaned and what cannot.

A damp basement also affects the floors above. Warm moist air rises, and joists in the ceiling can hold water. If the damage included ceilings and framing, see structural drying.

Restoration removes the water and dries the structure. It is not waterproofing. If water keeps coming back, a drainage or waterproofing contractor should look at the grading, gutters, sump system or foundation.

Common questions

Do you handle basement flooding emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and most basement floods start during storms. Same-day response comes when a team has time to reach you.

Is it safe to go into a flooded basement?

Not if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances. Leave and call the utility or 911 if there is an electrical or gas risk.

Will my sump pump stop a flood?

Not always. Pumps fail from power loss, clogs or too much water. A battery backup and a clear discharge help.

Is basement cleanup the same as waterproofing?

No. Cleanup removes water and dries the structure. Waterproofing addresses why water got in.
