AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Guilford, IN

Air conditioners make water every hour they run. When the condensate line clogs or a pan rusts through, that water goes into ceilings, attics and closets.

AC leak water damage is the moisture left after an air conditioner or heat pump releases condensate from a clogged drain line, a cracked pan or a failed coil. A typical system removes gallons of water from the air on a hot day, and when the drain path is blocked that water goes into the building.

In Guilford, the damage usually shows up as a ceiling stain, a wet closet or a damp attic. Turn the system off at the thermostat and call the 24/7 emergency line if water is dripping.

Shutting the system off stops new water from forming. Do not keep running a unit that is overflowing, and do not touch wet wiring near the air handler.

Why AC leaks go unnoticed

Condensate drains are small, often three-quarter inch pipes, and they clog with algae and dust. When one backs up, the secondary pan catches water for a while. If that overflows or has no float switch, the water has nowhere to go but the ceiling below or the floor around the air handler.

Many air handlers sit in attics, closets or crawl spaces. That is why the first sign is often a brown stain on a ceiling or a musty smell in a hallway. A stain in a ceiling below an attic unit is a common sign that the drain line has been dripping for days.

Clogged condensate line

Algae and debris block the drain. Water backs up and overflows the pan.

Rusted or cracked drain pan

Older metal pans corrode. Water drips through the bottom.

Frozen evaporator coil

A coil that freezes thaws and sends a lot of water at once. Low airflow and low refrigerant are the usual causes.

Failed float switch

The safety switch that should shut the system off did not trigger.

How the repair is handled

Restoration deals with the wet building materials. An HVAC technician fixes the cause, such as clearing the drain line or replacing the pan. Both jobs are needed, and the order depends on whether the system is still leaking. The first move is to stop the water, then dry what is wet.

1

Stop and locate

Shut off the system. Trace the water to the air handler, drain line or pan.

2

Remove wet materials

Wet insulation is removed. Wet drywall under the unit is cut out if it cannot be dried.

3

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the attic, closet or ceiling below. Moisture meters confirm progress.

4

Repair

Drywall, insulation and paint are replaced once the area is dry.

AC leaks are slow, which makes them Category 1 or 2 water, depending on how long the water sat and what it passed through. That makes timing important. A leak that ran for weeks often needs more removal than one caught on the first day. If the water has soaked insulation and framing, the work overlaps with ceiling water damage repair and structural drying.

Common questions

Why is my AC leaking water inside the house?

The most common cause is a clogged condensate drain line. Others are a rusted pan, a frozen coil or a failed float switch. An HVAC technician can confirm the cause.

Is AC water dangerous?

Condensate is usually not hazardous, but it can carry algae and grow mold if it sits in insulation or drywall. Dry the area quickly.

Should I keep running the AC if it leaks?

No. Turn it off until the drain is cleared. A running unit makes more water and worsens the damage.

Do you handle AC leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Guilford?

The line is open 24/7 for any active water. AC leaks are often not as urgent as a burst pipe, but a leak into a ceiling is worth a call right away.
