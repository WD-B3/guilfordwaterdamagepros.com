Prevent Water Damage While You Are Away From Home in Guilford, IN

By the Guilford Water Damage Pros team

A leak in an empty house runs for days before anyone sees it. A short checklist before you leave can cut that risk sharply.

By the teamGuilford, IN

The worst water damage often happens when nobody is home. A split supply hose or a failed water heater can run for days, soaking floors, walls and ceilings. A few minutes of preparation before a trip, a winter absence or a seasonal closing reduces the chance considerably. This checklist is arranged from most effective to least.

Before you leave

  1. Shut the main water valve if the house will be empty more than a few days.
  2. Replace old rubber washer hoses with braided stainless ones, and check ice maker and dishwasher lines.
  3. Turn the water heater to vacation mode or shut off its supply.
  4. Clear gutters and downspouts, and confirm they discharge away from the foundation.
  5. Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit, and confirm the discharge line is clear.

In cold weather

Leave the heat on at a safe setting, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and drain exposed lines if the house will be unheated. A frozen pipe can split without leaking until it thaws, so the flood begins when the weather warms. See frozen pipe water damage for how that plays out.

Have a person check in

Ask a neighbor, relative or property manager to look in at least weekly, with instructions on where the main valve is. Leave them the number for the emergency line and your insurer's claim line. Smart leak sensors under sinks and by the water heater can also send an alert to your phone.

Insurers often have rules about unoccupied homes. The Indiana Department of Insurance says your policy might require notification of a loss within a certain time frame, and some policies limit coverage when a house has been vacant for a stretch. Ask your agent before a long absence.

Guilford away-from-home facts

Guilford sits at the forks of Tanners Creek, and a heavy rain while you are away can bring water to a basement or crawl space with nobody there to see it. A working sump pump with a battery backup is a sensible check before travel in this part of Dearborn County.

The climate in the county is humid subtropical, with hot, humid summers and generally mild to cool winters, according to the Moores Hill entry. A summer absence brings AC condensate risk, and a winter absence brings freeze risk.

Appliances and fixtures worth checking twice a year

  • Washing machine hoses: look for bulges, cracks and rust at the couplings, and replace them every few years.
  • Water heater: check the tank for rust streaks, the drain pan for water and the relief valve for drips.
  • Dishwasher and refrigerator lines: pull the unit out slightly and look for moisture on the floor.
  • Toilet supply lines and shutoff valves: check for slow drips and mineral crust.
  • AC drain line and pan: flush the line each season and confirm the float switch works.

A leak that is found during a quick check costs a part and an hour. The same leak found after a week away costs floors, drywall and drying time.

Write down where each shutoff is and tell everyone who might need it. In an emergency, finding the main valve should take seconds, not minutes.

A one-page pre-trip card

Write a short card and leave it on the counter for whoever checks in. It should list where the main valve is, where the breaker panel is, the emergency line for water, your insurer's claim line and your own phone number. Add the water heater's location and any special notes, such as a sump pump that needs checking.

Take a minute before you lock the door to scan each room for drips and damp spots, and photograph the house as a record of its condition.

Plan for power outages too

A storm that knocks out power can stop a sump pump and let water in. A battery backup or a water-powered backup pump keeps it running. Test the battery each season and replace it on schedule. If you leave for a long time during storm season, ask a neighbor to check the basement after heavy rain.

Common questions

Should I shut off the main water when going on vacation?

For more than a few days, yes. It removes the pressure that drives a flood.

Do smart leak sensors help?

They can alert you early, but they work best with someone nearby who can act on the alert.

What if I come home and the floor is wet in Guilford?

Shut the main valve, avoid wet outlets and call the 24/7 line. Photograph before you move things.

Can an insurer deny a claim for a vacant house?

Some policies limit vacancy coverage. Ask your agent before a long absence.
